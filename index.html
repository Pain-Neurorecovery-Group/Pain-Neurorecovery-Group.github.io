About The Pain-Neurorecovery-Group

The Pain & Neurorecovery Group is part of the University Clinic for Neurorehabilitation at Aarhus University and is based at Hammel Neurocenter, one of Denmark’s two highly specialized national neurorehabilitation centers for people with acquired brain injury. The group brings together experimental, translational, and clinical research. Our work encompasses psychophysics, computational modelling, clinical neurophysiology, clinical trials, and clinical data science. We also operate the Hammel Pain Physiology Lab, an embedded hospital-based laboratory that provides patients with convenient, barrier-free access to experimental pain studies and clinical trials.

Research

  • Our research

    Our research focuses on acquired and early-life neurological conditions affecting the central nervous system, including spinal cord injury, stroke, traumatic brain injury, and cerebral palsy. Although these conditions form the core of our work, we also collaborate with national and international partners on related disorders. These collaborations include research on Guillain–Barré syndrome with the Danish Pain Research Center at Aarhus University and research on complex regional pain syndrome with the Movement Disorder and Pain Research Center at Rigshospitalet Glostrup and the Spinal Cord Injury Center at Balgrist University Hospital, University of Zurich. A central aim of our research is to bridge the translational gap between experimental studies in healthy volunteers and clinical research in patients. In healthy volunteers, we develop and validate methods and improve our understanding of pain processing, thermal stimulus representation, and neurophysiological mechanisms. In clinical populations, we apply these methods to investigate pain mechanisms and neurological recovery in real-world disease contexts. Using psychophysical paradigms, pupillometry, evoked potentials, motor unit number estimation, and sympathetic skin responses, we combine experimental and clinical neurophysiology with clinical data science. This integrated approach enables us to identify predictors and potential confounders of recovery trajectories and to translate mechanistic findings into clinically relevant knowledge.
